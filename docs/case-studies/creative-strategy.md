# Creative strategy: Meta ads into VSL and webinar funnels

I wrote the ad hooks and managed the Meta campaigns for three coaching offers, and tested more than 150 ad creatives across those accounts. The numbers below come from the clients' Hyros dashboards over 30 days from September 30, 2026. Clients aren't named here; the dashboard screenshots are available on request.

## Results

| Offer | Spend | Revenue | ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Airbnb coaching, VSL and webinar funnels | $101,540 | $506,790 | 4.99 |
| Airbnb coaching | A$286,471 | A$861,976 (234 sales) | 3.0 |
| Co-living arbitrage coaching | A$15,972 | A$122,938 | 7.7 |

ROAS is Hyros's own figure (attributed revenue divided by ad spend), so it counts sales Hyros tied to an ad, not every sale the offer made that month.

## Where my angles come from

Every hook started from something a buyer said, not from a brainstorm:

- **Reviews and testimonials.** Student wins and reviews gave me the outcomes buyers cared about, in their own words.
- **Comments on ads and posts.** Objections and doubts in the comments became hooks that answered them before the click.
- **Sales calls and DMs.** What prospects said on calls and in DMs showed which promises closed and which ones they didn't believe.
- **Competitor ads.** The Meta Ad Library showed which angles the market had already worn out, so I could pick a different one.

## What I did

- **Wrote the hooks.** Every ad opened on a hook I wrote for that offer's buyer, then pointed into either the VSL or the webinar funnel.
- **Managed the campaigns.** Launching, budgets, and deciding which ads stayed on and which came off, inside Meta Ads Manager.
- **Tested 150+ creatives** across the three accounts, so decisions came from what the ads did, not from what I liked.
- **Read results through Hyros**, so a winning ad meant attributed sales, not cheap clicks.

## How I judge a test

These are the rules I hold myself to before calling an ad a winner or a loser:

- **Give the ad set room to learn.** Meta needs about 50 conversion events per ad set in 7 days to leave the learning phase, so a test runs on one ad set and nothing gets edited until it's out.
- **"Still learning" is not "not working."** I judge an ad on cost per sale against the offer's break-even, not on how it looks on day two.
- **Check the event fires first.** Before reading any result, I confirm the purchase or booking event is actually reaching Meta, because a broken event makes every ad look bad.
- **Set the test budget before spending.** Each offer gets a test budget agreed up front, and a test that loses past it gets stopped.

## Where it connects to the rest of my work

The same habits run through my outbound work: A/B copy testing across 225 sending inboxes, scoring every list before a send, and building the tooling instead of doing it by hand. I keep ad briefs in the client portal I built, and I use Claude for research and first drafts, with every claim checked before it ships.
